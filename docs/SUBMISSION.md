**Description (draft, < 500 words):**

> NEXUS is an agentic spatial intelligence platform for scientific discovery on Meta Quest. A scientist sits at their desk, a real piece of the Moon — built from NASA LOLA elevation data and LROC imagery — appears in passthrough, and they investigate it with their hands and voice.
>
> Point at a feature and describe it ("boulder field here, blocks look angular"). NEXUS resolves what "here" means from your hand ray, gaze and words, records an anchored observation, and dispatches specialist AI agents. You watch them fly to the site: an elevation analyst measures depth from real terrain data, an imagery analyst reads the orbital image, a geology researcher proposes interpretations, and a skeptic looks for evidence against them.
>
> Before anything reaches you, our Scientific Integrity Engine checks every claim. Evidence IDs must exist, numbers must match measurements computed in code, hypotheses must be labelled as hypotheses, and contradictions must be shown. Claims that fail are removed — and shown as removed. The same engine governs speech: NEXUS never *sounds* more certain than its evidence, so a hypothesis is always spoken as "one possibility, with high uncertainty…".
>
> Findings appear anchored to the terrain and are spoken by each agent from where it's working, using spatial audio. Ask "what contradicts this?" or "where does that come from?" and get answers grounded only in the investigation's evidence. Start an Evidence Debate and hear two hypotheses argue while you moderate. Then approve, reject or mark the finding inconclusive — by poke or by voice — and your reasoning is stored verbatim in the logbook.
>
> Everything can be done with hands alone; voice makes it faster and more accessible, with live captions and multilingual support.
>
> The Moon is the first environment. The same architecture — agents, integrity engine, spatial voice — extends to oceans, archaeology, volcanoes and Mars.

**Hand interactions (optional field):**
> Ray + pinch to select distant features; poke for cards and chips; pinch-drag to rotate the terrain; two-hand pinch to zoom; grab to move the tabletop model; palm-up wrist menu with pinch-and-hold push-to-talk. Every voice action has a hand equivalent, and a complete investigation can be done with the microphone off.
